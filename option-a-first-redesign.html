The problem

A federal zone only goes so far.

Federal policymakers increasingly use special zones to direct investment toward national priorities. Opportunity Zones are one familiar example. But permitting, siting, tax treatment, workforce rules, and utility and land-use decisions are largely made by states and localities.

Too often the sequence runs backward. A zone is designated, then states scramble to respond while projects stall and investors wait. States that are ready in advance are better positioned to attract designation, and to make it work once it arrives.

The idea

A standing invitation.

A Mirror Zone is a state-level framework that mirrors the shape of a federal priority zone before one exists in that state. The state adopts a standard package of supportive rules, written to switch on automatically when a matching federal designation arrives.

The framework is deliberately agnostic to what is being built. It is a reusable template that can be pointed at any national priority.

How it works

Three steps, one template.

STEP 01

Mirror

The state enacts a defined package of supportive rules now, keyed to a federal designation by reference rather than restating federal details.

STEP 02

Invite

The state signals to federal decision-makers and to investors that it is ready, and makes its case for designation.

STEP 03

Build

When a designation arrives, the rules activate and projects get a clearer, faster path to completion.

The toolkit

What a Mirror Zone contains.

Examples of the kinds of priorities the model can serve. Which federal zones exist, on what terms, and where they are designated is decided by federal law and agencies. Mirroring does not guarantee designation.
